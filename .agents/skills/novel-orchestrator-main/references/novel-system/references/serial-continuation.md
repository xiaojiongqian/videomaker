# Serial Continuation

Use this reference when a project is not fully planned from the beginning, when a story keeps extending into sequels, or when the user wants to keep the world open.

## Principle

Open-ended serial writing is not failed outlining. Treat it as rolling commitment:

- Commit hard to what has reached the page or has been explicitly confirmed.
- Keep future possibilities alive as labeled candidates.
- Let each new installment create its own dramatic engine instead of merely servicing old setup.

## Horizon Model

Maintain three horizons:

- `now`
  - Current chapter/scene pressure, core event, immediate cost, and ending residue.
- `near`
  - Obligations likely to matter in the next 1 to 3 chapters: open loops, relationship debts, visible consequences, promised meetings, deadlines, injuries, secrets known by specific people.
- `far`
  - Sequel seeds, active unknowns, dormant antagonisms, institutions, places, objects, myths, and relationship cracks that can wait.

Never force `far` material into `now` unless it increases the current scene's pressure.

## Continuation Handshake

Before a sequel or new long segment, produce:

- `stable_end_state`
  - Where each central character stands after the previous installment.
- `unpaid_costs`
  - Consequences that still affect behavior or options.
- `open_loops`
  - Mark each as `must_pay`, `can_delay`, `can_reframe`, or `can_retire`.
- `active_unknowns`
  - Things deliberately unknown inside the story.
- `sequel_seeds`
  - Candidate pressures, places, objects, relationships, or antagonistic systems.
- `new_entry_disturbance`
  - A fresh event or pressure that makes the new installment worth reading on its own.

## Candidate Discipline

Use explicit labels:

- `canon`
  - Confirmed by stable text or explicit user decision.
- `state`
  - Current dynamic condition confirmed by text.
- `candidate`
  - Plausible future idea; not yet true.
- `seed`
  - Reusable pressure source that may pay off later.
- `active_unknown`
  - A deliberate blank that should not be filled prematurely.
- `retired`
  - A loop or candidate deliberately dropped or replaced.

When writing state files, keep candidates out of canon sections.

## Sequel Entry Rules

A sequel should inherit consequences, not a checklist.

Good sequel openings usually do at least two of these:

- Show the cost of the previous ending in a concrete situation.
- Move a dormant seed into immediate pressure.
- Introduce a new disturbance that reframes old relationships.
- Give the protagonist a new pursuit under changed constraints.
- Make an old victory create a fresh vulnerability.

Avoid openings that only recap lore, explain the whole new arc, or pay off setup before it has pressure.

## Infinite Extension Guardrails

- Do not close every loop just because a volume ends.
- Do not keep every loop alive just because it once existed.
- Retire loops that no longer generate pressure.
- Split `mystery` from `confusion`: the reader may lack answers, but should understand who wants what and what changes now.
- Let canon accrete from scenes; do not let a future masterplan overwrite already stable character behavior.
- Prefer renewable tensions: relationships, institutions, recurring places, public reputation, debt, secrets, wounds, promises, and objects with memory.

## Output Additions

For open-ended continuation tasks, include:

- `continuation_handshake`
- `now_near_far_horizon`
- `sequel_seeds`
- `carry_forward_obligations`
- `retired_or_deferred_loops`
- `proposed_writebacks`
