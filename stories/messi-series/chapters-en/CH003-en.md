# CH003 One Week Later

On April 7, 2001, the day of the match against Amposta, Messi finally saw his name on the official team sheet. He had stared at that piece of paper too many times before, but this time it was different. He looked at his own name for two extra seconds, as if afraid it might suddenly vanish. In the dressing room, someone handed him the number 9 shirt and said, "You're wearing this today." Messi took it, pressed his fingers once against the number, and said only, "Got it." His voice was flat, but he pulled the shirt on very quickly.

Only that afternoon did six months of waiting finally become a real match. Barcelona beat Amposta 3-0, and Messi scored one of the goals. When the chance came, he was there first, driving in and sending the ball into the net. Even his celebration seemed to arrive late. His teammates were already flying at him while he still looked as if he had not fully caught up with what had happened. One patted the back of his head, another tugged his shirt and dragged him back, and people on the touchline were shouting his name. The months when he had not been allowed to play official matches, the weekends when he had stayed behind in the building alone, all of them were cut off by that one goal.

After the match, the look on Jorge's face loosened a little. As father and son walked out together, Jorge said, "At least you've finally played now." Messi threw his bag onto his shoulder. He was still breathing hard, but he answered at once, "I have to keep going." He did not say much, but the force of it was all there on his face. Once a door had opened, no one was going to be satisfied just standing in the doorway.

The second official match came quickly. On April 14, against Tortosa, Barcelona eventually won 5-1, but Messi could not carry the good start from the previous week forward. After one challenge, he landed awkwardly. The outside of his lower leg seemed to give way at once, and the whole leg immediately lost its strength. He tried to take another step, but he could not stay upright. People on the touchline ran over and asked if he could continue. He gritted his teeth, shook his head, and forced out a quiet answer: "I can't." A fractured fibula. The match had not even finished, and he had already been carried off.

In the space of one week, everything had turned over completely. The week before, he had just put on the number 9 shirt and just put his name into an official match record. This week, he needed crutches just to stand up and walk. The others kept training, kept playing, kept appearing on team sheets. He could only sit down, prop up his leg, and watch time crawl by. The worst part was not the pain. It was the stopping. The seats in the stands were not far from the pitch, yet they felt far enough to make a person suffocate. When teammates went out to warm up, some would look up and wave at him. After games, others would run over and ask, "How are you?" Messi always gave the same reply: "I'm fine."

Saying he was fine did not mean he really felt that way. One day, after watching an entire match and waiting until almost everyone had left, he turned to Jorge and asked, "When can I run again?" Jorge looked at him and did not offer one of those empty comforting lines. He only said, "First let the injury heal." Messi said nothing after that, but his eyes stayed fixed on the field. For other people, recovery might have been nothing more than time. For him, it was a stretch of days in which he had been forcibly held down.

But when the injury healed, he went back to the training ground. In the beginning, people watched his first few steps closely, afraid he would rush it. Someone reminded him, "Take it slow." Messi nudged the ball forward lightly, looked up, and answered, "I know." Then on the next touch he was already after it. He had played only two official matches for Barcelona's youth side before the second one was interrupted by a fracture. Now that the injury had healed, what he had to do was also clear: first get back into matches, then reconnect the road that had broken off after only one week.
