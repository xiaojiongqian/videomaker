# CH002 Outside the List

Before leaving, Jorge sat for a long time in that room at the old La Masia. The door was not fully shut. People ran past outside, and their footsteps kept striking the floor in waves. Then he suddenly asked, "Do you want to go back?" Messi was bent over, untying his boots. His hands stopped for a second. He looked up and said, "No. I want to stay here." Then he lowered his head and kept tying the laces, as if the sentence did not need a second explanation.

That was nearly two months after the promise on the napkin had been written down, and only then did Messi truly move into the old La Masia building beside Camp Nou. The building was old, the staircase narrow, so narrow that two people walking side by side would have found it tight. The corridor was long, with doors lined one after another, and around the corner there was someone else's room, then the bathroom, then the dining hall. There was not much luggage: one suitcase, a few clothes, a few pairs of boots. What was really hard to carry was not any of that. It was the fact that from that day on he had to learn how to stay here.

Everything was different from Rosario. The sound of the wake-up bell was different. The smell in the dining hall was different. Even the speed at which the boys in the corridor spoke was different. In those first days after moving in, Messi would often stop at the doorway for a moment before deciding which way to go.

What old La Masia put in front of him first was not match time. It was a whole set of rules that had to be learned immediately. When to get up. When to go downstairs. When to head to the training ground. When to return to the residence. When lights went out. Every one of those things was watched. Messi did not talk much, but he learned those rules quickly. He learned which turn on the staircase came fastest, when the dining hall was most crowded, and where he could steal a little more time with the ball after training. The place did not feel like home yet, but it had already begun to grow the shape of a daily life.

What was truly hard was this: he had moved in, but official matches still were not for him. The transfer and registration papers were delayed again and again. For six months he could only train and play a few friendlies. Every weekend the squad list would be posted on the same wall. Other boys would change quickly and run downstairs. Messi would always go over first and look. The names ran down the page line after line, and his was not there. He would scan from the first line to the last, then lower his head and read it again. Someone would lean over his shoulder and say, "Still not there." Messi would smooth the edge of the page with one hand and ask quietly, "When will it be there?" The other boy would shrug. "Don't know. You'll have to wait."

The days of waiting for official eligibility felt especially long inside old La Masia. When the others left for matches, he would drift back to the edge of the training pitch, or pin the ball to his foot and take it on a few more runs by himself. On an emptier stretch of concrete or grass, the sound of studs scraping the ground could seem strangely loud. Sometimes, when the weekend crowd had all gone out, the building would suddenly fall quiet, as if every bit of noise had run down the stairs and toward the stadium, leaving only him behind. Messi did not complain. He did not make a scene. He only nudged the ball forward and chased after it. He believed one thing and one thing only: if he could still train like this, then the story was not over yet.

The boys his age did slowly gather around him. Pique was tall and loud, and when he saw how quiet Messi was, he would deliberately wave a hand in front of his face and say, "Argentine, do you actually know how to talk?" Fabregas saved him a seat in the dining hall sometimes, tapping the table and saying, "Here." Toni Calvo, Marc Valiente, and others would stay a little longer after training too and knock the ball around with him. Once Pique teased him again: "You really can't say one more sentence?" Messi stopped the ball by his foot, lifted his head, and said, "Let's just play." It was a short answer, but the boys around him all laughed. The jokes inside the old building were not always gentle, but they at least made the place feel like more than rules and waiting.

And so the weeks kept dragging by. The same staircase. The same list. The same weekends arriving, and still his name not on the page. But Messi no longer stood in the doorway staring blankly as he had at first. He knew where the training ground was. He knew who would save a seat for him. He knew whether going downstairs a little later would still leave enough room to get touches on the ball. And he knew one thing very clearly: he was not going back to Rosario.

Official clearance had still not come through.

But he had already settled one part of the matter in his own head: he would not go back. He would stay here, wait out the six months, and wait until that list finally carried his name for the first time.

## Important Characters

### Lionel Messi
- Summary: Inside the old La Masia, he says he wants to stay for real, even while official matches remain out of reach.
- Current Status: He is inside the system now and has chosen Barcelona in his own mind, but match eligibility is still trapped behind paperwork and waiting.

### Jorge Messi
- Summary: Father and keeper of the possible way back, the one who is willing to ask whether they should return home.
- Current Status: He is still handling the practical side of exile while watching his son choose to stay anyway.

### Gerard Pique
- Summary: A representative of the boys around Messi, helping La Masia feel like more than rules and distance.
- Current Status: Along with the other boys, he is making the wait outside the official list a little more livable.
