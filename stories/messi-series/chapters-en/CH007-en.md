# CH007 The One Who Ran the Whole Length of the Pitch

## Stamford Bridge Pushed Him into View

In February 2006, the thing in front of Messi was no longer a youth-team list or a late substitution board in league play. It was the Champions League round of sixteen. On February 22, the first leg arrived at Stamford Bridge. Barcelona were away to Chelsea, on a pitch cut up by studs and heavy with mud, against Mourinho's team, where the rhythm was hard and the physical contact harder. But Messi did not take a step back first. From the opening minutes he kept demanding the ball high up the pitch. Even when the grass would not quite obey the ball, he still drove into crowded spaces. In the first half, Del Horno went in too hard and was eventually sent off for fouling Messi. Barcelona won the first leg 2-1 and carried the advantage back to Camp Nou. The away win mattered, of course. But what more people began to remember was this eighteen-year-old Argentine: even on the hardest ground in a Champions League knockout tie, he still ran straight into the crowd.

Two weeks later, the second leg returned to Camp Nou. Barcelona had the 2-1 lead from the first leg, and if they could simply hold the home match together, they would reach the quarter-finals. In the 25th minute, Messi made a forward burst, then suddenly grabbed at his right thigh. He tried to take one more step, but the leg would not answer. When the medical staff reached him, he was already shaking his head. "No." He was substituted and had to watch the rest of the match from the side. The post-match result was simple and brutal: a right thigh muscle injury, at least four weeks out. The game ended 1-1, Barcelona advanced 3-2 on aggregate, and the quarter-finals, the semi-finals, and the Paris final against Arsenal all went on without him. The same tie that first shoved him into the center of a European big night also brought the injury that took the bigger stage away.

That injury brought his spring of 2006 to an early end. By the time he returned to the center of Champions League nights and clasicos in the spring of 2007, his place in those games had changed. On March 10, 2007, Real Madrid came to Camp Nou. Before half an hour had passed, Raul and Van Nistelrooy had both scored for Madrid, and twice they had pulled the score away. Twice Messi had dragged it back, and the game was quickly 2-2. Before halftime, Oleguer was sent off, and Barcelona had to play the second half with ten men, defending and waiting for one last chance. Near the end, Ramos made it 3-2 and the whole stadium tightened at once. In stoppage time, the ball found Messi again, and he sent the last shot into the net. 3-3. At nineteen, Messi scored the first hat-trick of his Barcelona career, and he did it in the biggest match of all. By then he was no longer the boy waiting for the substitution board. He was the player who could rewrite the match from the opening whistle.

## The Run That Began at Midfield

Just over a month later, on April 18, Barcelona met Getafe in the first leg of the Copa del Rey semi-final. It was a two-legged tie. Barcelona were at home first, and if they wanted the second leg to feel lighter, they needed to build the advantage here. Xavi made it 1-0 in the 18th minute. In the 29th, the ball reached Messi near the halfway line. He touched it forward once, then once more, and when the speed rose, his body seemed to flatten against the grass and cut forward. The first defender was gone. The second lunged and missed. The third turned too late. By the time Messi carried the ball into the box, the goalkeeper Luis Garcia could only rush out.

In Messi's head there was only the goal now. He had glimpsed Eto'o once, but the recovering defenders were already closing the space and there was only enough time for him to finish the move himself. So he knocked the ball on again, went around the goalkeeper, and from an angle that had become very narrow, still sent it into the net. Almost the whole Camp Nou rose to its feet in the same instant. Barcelona won 5-2 that night, and Messi scored another before halftime too, but what everyone remembered more fiercely was that run from near the halfway line. The next day the papers and the television reports were saying the same thing: it looked too much like Maradona's 1986 World Cup goal against England.

But a Copa semi-final has two legs, and the noise from the first one could not finish the story by itself. On May 10, Barcelona traveled to Getafe with a 5-2 lead that seemed heavy enough to carry them through. Yet once the match began, the game tilted little by little the other way. Getafe scored, then scored again, and Barcelona never truly settled it. They lost the second leg 4-0. The three-goal advantage from the first leg was wiped away, and Barcelona went out 6-5 on aggregate.

## After the Miracle, the Road Was Not Finished

That made his situation in those two years even clearer. Against Chelsea in 2006, he had first been seen by all of Europe in a Champions League knockout tie, and then lost the bigger stage to injury. In 2007, the clasico and the first leg against Getafe pushed his name higher still.

Being seen was no longer the problem.

What waited for him now was no longer how to make people remember him, but how to press those huge nights one by one into results that actually lasted.

## Important Characters

### Lionel Messi
- Summary: From Stamford Bridge to Getafe, he begins to be asked for answers on the biggest nights.
- Age & Appearance: 18 to 19; still short but more powerfully built, quicker off the mark, already unmistakable as dark-haired, head down, left foot close to the ball.
- Current Status: He is no longer just a young substitute, yet injury and team results still cling tightly to every miracle.

### Xavi
- Summary: The midfielder who keeps Barcelona's attacking structure legible and makes Messi's decisive touches land inside a pattern.
- Age & Appearance: 26 to 27; medium-built, short dark hair, clean facial lines, with a stance and turn that always look precise.
- Current Status: He remains a crucial part of the order that lets Messi's moments truly change matches.

### Barcelona
- Summary: The team lifted by Messi's breakthrough moments and still forced to absorb the cost of unfinished growth.
- Age & Appearance: Age not applicable; visually a high, bright line of red and blue, glamorous on the surface and still carrying a little unfinished youth underneath.
- Current Status: It has seen him decide the biggest games, but it also knows that one miracle does not solve everything that follows.
