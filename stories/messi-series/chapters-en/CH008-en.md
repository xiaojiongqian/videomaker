# CH008 The Question the Ballon d'Or Could Not Answer

## The Number 10 and Olympic Gold Arrived Together

On July 24, 2008, before a friendly against Hibernian, Messi wore Barcelona's number 10 for the first time. On August 3, the club announced it officially: the shirt was his. The number reached his back first. Its weight had not fully settled there yet. A month later, in the men's Olympic final against Nigeria, it was the blue and white shirt that put a real final under his feet first.

There were 89,102 people inside the Bird's Nest. In the 58th minute, the score was still 0-0. Nigeria were sitting deep, not in disorder, but the line in front of them still had not truly been broken. Messi received the ball in the final third, lifted his head once, and slipped it through. Di Maria ran diagonally across the line and chipped it over the goalkeeper before he could get there.

1-0.

That pass cut the whole final open. The remaining half hour suddenly became very long. Argentina had to protect not just a scoreline, but the last gate of the entire tournament. When the whistle went, Messi had finally lifted a real major medal with the blue and white shirt. But the first question that gold medal answered belonged only to that youth national-team level.

## Rome Made the Answer More Complete

Once the number 10 was on his back, the thing that truly gave it weight was the season that followed. By the spring of 2009, the pressure had shifted to Europe. In the Champions League semi-final, Barcelona drew 0-0 with Chelsea at Camp Nou in the first leg. The second leg at Stamford Bridge went worse: Chelsea went ahead, and later Barcelona were down to ten men. In stoppage time, a clearance dropped back near the edge of the box. Messi did not rush his own shot. He rolled it sideways to Iniesta instead. 1-1. Barcelona kept Rome alive on away goals. When the door was almost closed, he was the one who held it open.

On May 27, the final against Manchester United was played at the Stadio Olimpico in Rome. Eto'o made it 1-0 in the 10th minute, but a lead that narrow could not secure a match like this. The moment that truly locked the final came in the 70th. Xavi lifted a cross from the right. Messi made his run through the middle, rose up, and headed it in.

2-0.

That goal was almost the least Messi-like thing about him. It was not a low dribble, not a sudden cut, not a dart through traffic before a finish tucked into the corner. It was a header. And precisely because it was unlike him, it felt even more like a seal. When the whistle blew, the league, the Copa del Rey, and the Champions League all belonged to Barcelona. Spanish football had never seen one club collect all three in the same season before. By then, the weight the number 10 was meant to carry on the club side had almost all been pressed down by him.

## After the Ballon d'Or, the Blue and White Remained

On December 1, the Ballon d'Or vote pinned that year down once more. At twenty-two, Messi won 473 points, 240 ahead of Cristiano Ronaldo in second place. The number 10, the gold medal in Beijing, that stoppage-time pass before Rome, and the header in Rome that seemed least like him, were all laid side by side in a single year. A ballot sheet could write all that down very clearly. But no matter how brightly the Ballon d'Or shone, it could only illuminate the club side more fully.

The next time people looked at Messi, they would not be looking only at the number 10 on his back.

They would be looking first at the scoreboard beside Argentina's blue and white shirt.

## Important Characters

### Lionel Messi
- Summary: The player who takes Barcelona's 10, wins Olympic gold, keeps Stamford Bridge alive, seals Rome, and wins the Ballon d'Or.
- Age & Appearance: 21 to 22; stronger and tighter through the frame, still youthful in the face, short dark hair, and looking more like the center of the team once the number 10 is his.
- Current Status: His authority on the club side has hardened, but that only leaves Argentina's question waiting for him more directly.

### Angel Di Maria
- Summary: The runner who turns Messi's through ball into Olympic gold.
- Age & Appearance: 20; slender and long-limbed, narrow-faced, light through the legs, cutting diagonally like a line that suddenly opens space.
- Current Status: He has completed the final touch in Beijing and helped give the blue-and-white shirt one real answer.

### Andres Iniesta
- Summary: The finisher at Stamford Bridge who turns Messi's last rescue pass into survival.
- Age & Appearance: 24 to 25; slim, with very short hair and a low center of gravity that makes every turn and first touch look light.
- Current Status: He has received the ball at the final second and pulled Barcelona back from a door that was almost shut.
