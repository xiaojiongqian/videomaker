# CH004 The Season Behind the Mask

Once the fibula fracture from the spring of 2001 had healed, Messi no longer needed crutches. On his first day back with Barcelona U14A, he kept the ball close at first, as if testing whether that leg would still obey him. Someone at the side reminded him, "Slow down." Messi nodded, and with his very next touch still pushed the ball out in front of him. After an injury, the hardest part is that every movement begins with the same question: will this one be stopped again too? He had fought his way back from the stands to the grass. He did not want to be pushed backward again.

The 2001/02 season that followed mattered especially to him. The previous year, he had managed to play only two official matches before the second one ended in a fracture. This year, he could finally stay with U14A from start to finish and play a whole season through. When the weekend came, his name was on the list. When the match began, he was still there. After training, when his teammates headed back toward the dressing room, he often stayed by the goal for a few extra shots. Someone would call out, "That's enough." Messi would stop the ball, look at the goal, and say, "One last one." Then, before the sentence had even settled, he was off again.

The most important thing about that season was that it did not break. He scored today, then again the next week. He was on the list for this match, and he was still on it for the next one. When the coaches looked at the reports afterward, they kept finding the same name in the line for goals. By the time the season was drawing to a close, the numbers had become impossible to miss: 38 goals in 31 matches, including four hat-tricks, one four-goal match, and nine braces. But what his teammates tended to remember was often not the total. It was something simpler and more direct: if the ball reached the final third, Messi still wanted one more go.

As the season moved on, the Catalan Cup moved toward its final too. Before the match against Espanyol, the injury to Messi's face had not fully healed, so the team found a protective mask for him. A black strap wrapped around the back of his head, and once it was fastened, half his face felt trapped inside it. Before he went on, someone tightened the strap for him and asked, "Can you do it?" Messi raised a hand and touched the mask. "I can play."

But once the match started, he knew the thing was wrong. His breathing felt blocked, his sight felt awkward, and even when the ball stayed right by his foot, he had the sense that he was still half a beat late. After only seven minutes, he gestured toward the touchline. By the time a staff member got close, Messi had already reached up to his face. "Take it off."

The moment the strap loosened and the mask came away, it felt as if the breath caught in his chest came free with it. The game opened up again. Messi trusted himself to accelerate, trusted himself to drive into bodies again. Before halftime was over, he had scored twice. Teammates flew at him again, and Barcelona went into the break 3-0 up. The mask lay by the touchline where it had been set down, and no one tried to fasten it onto him again. Barcelona went on to win the final 4-1 and collected the cup as well.

That final made the change in his year perfectly clear. The year before, injury had yanked him out of official football. This year, he had completed a full U14A season and written his name into the scoresheet again and again. Coaches from U15 and higher levels had started coming to the touchline to watch this small left-footed boy, though an official promotion had not happened yet. For Messi, the next step was also clear: take the continuity he had built in this season and carry it upward, then knock on the next door.
