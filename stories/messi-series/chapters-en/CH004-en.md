# CH004 The Season Behind the Mask

## First, a Full Season

Once the fibula fracture from the spring of 2001 had healed, Messi no longer needed crutches. On his first day back with Barcelona U14A, he kept the ball close at first, as if testing whether that leg would still obey him. Someone at the side reminded him, "Slow down." Messi nodded, and with his very next touch still pushed the ball out in front of him. After an injury, the hardest part is not the pain. It is that every movement begins with the same question: will this one be stopped again too? He had fought his way back from the stands to the grass. He did not want to be pushed backward again.

The most important thing about the 2001/02 season that followed was that it did not break. The previous year, he had managed to play only two official matches before the second one ended in a fracture. This year, the team sheet stabilized, and the matches linked together week after week. When training ended and the others headed toward the dressing room, he often stayed by the goal for a few extra shots. Someone would call out, "That's enough." Messi would stop the ball, look at the goal, and say, "One last one." Then, before the sentence had even settled, he was off again. By the time the season was drawing to a close, the numbers were impossible to miss: 38 goals in 31 matches, including four hat-tricks, one four-goal match, and nine braces. But in the end, the weight of that whole season would be pressed into one final.

## The Mask Lasted Seven Minutes

Before the Catalan Cup final against Espanyol, the injury to Messi's face had not fully healed, so the team found him a protective mask that Puyol had used before. A black strap wrapped around the back of his head, and once it was fastened, half his face felt trapped inside it. Before he went on, someone tightened the strap for him and asked, "Can you do it?" Messi raised a hand and touched the mask. "I can play."

But once the match started, he knew the thing was wrong. His breathing felt blocked, his sight felt awkward, and even when the ball stayed right by his foot, he still felt half a beat late. After only seven minutes, he gestured toward the touchline. By the time a staff member got close, Messi had already reached up to his face. "Take it off."

The moment the strap loosened and the mask came away, it felt as if the breath caught in his chest came free with it. The game opened up again. Messi trusted himself to accelerate, trusted himself to drive into bodies again. Before halftime was over, he had scored twice. Teammates flew at him, and Barcelona went into the break 3-0 up. The mask lay by the touchline where it had been set down, and no one tried to fasten it onto him again. Barcelona went on to win the final 4-1 and collected the cup as well.

## This Level Was Already Too Small

That one match tied the previous year and this year together at once. The year before, injury had yanked him out of official football. This year, not even an unfinished facial injury and a mask strapped across his face could push him back to the side again. When the match ended, a few people who did not usually belong to U14A were still standing there by the pitch. Their eyes did not follow the trophy very far. They settled first on the boy who had just taken the mask off.

This level was already beginning to feel too small to hold him.

The only thing that had not arrived yet was the official word sending him upward.

## Important Characters

### Lionel Messi
- Summary: The boy who finally completes a full season after injury and turns continuity into goals again.
- Age & Appearance: 14 to 15; still slight in frame, but with more strength beginning to grow in the legs, youthful in the face, and especially bright-eyed once the mask is strapped on.
- Current Status: He has taken the mask off, finished the season, and is beginning to look too large for this level to hold.

### Barcelona U14A
- Summary: The team that gives Messi his competitive rhythm back after the injury break.
- Age & Appearance: Age not applicable; a whole row of red-and-blue youth bodies, more settled in rhythm and more capable of taking him back in.
- Current Status: It has watched him turn a full season and a final into an unmistakable sign of upward movement.
