# CH013 The Morning After the World Cup

## Quito Kicked the Door Open First

On October 10, 2017, in Quito, Ecuador scored before the first minute had even properly finished. The blow arrived so quickly that it seemed to spill open everything Argentina had failed to steady over the previous year. Messi had pulled himself back from the edge of departure in 2016, but the return had not made the national team smoother. Bauza was gone by April. Jorge Sampaoli arrived in June. The points table in South American qualifying was still balanced in the most dangerous place. By the last matchday, Argentina had no room left behind them.

That was why the game in Quito never felt like an ordinary away qualifier. It felt like a rope pulled as tight as it could go. After the early goal, there was no chance to let the rhythm return slowly. Everything had to be pushed back under Messi's feet again. He scored three times, turned a 0-1 hole into a 3-1 win, and dragged Argentina into Russia. But what remained from that night was not only a hat-trick. It was the heavier fact beneath it: if this team wanted to reach the World Cup at all, it still needed him to kick the door open first.

## Russia Did Not Make the Weight Lighter

By June 2018, the door did open onto Russia. The problem was that it did not feel any lighter on the other side.

Against Iceland in the first group match, Argentina went ahead through Sergio Aguero and were quickly pulled back to 1-1. Then Messi stepped to the penalty spot and saw Hannes Halldorsson save it. The chill that came from that moment was familiar at once. Four years earlier in Brazil, the question had been why the last step would not come. Four years later in Russia, even the first step seemed unable to sit firmly beneath Argentina's feet.

The second match, against Croatia, collapsed even faster. At 0-0, the game still looked as if it might be worked loose bit by bit. Then Willy Caballero's mistake lifted the ball into the air, Ante Rebic struck it on the way down, and the whole road tilted. Luka Modric scored. Ivan Rakitic scored. The match stopped at 0-3. It was more than a defeat. It looked like an entire team loosening in front of the world.

That left Nigeria.

Messi was the one who first tied the loose line together again. Ever Banega's long pass dropped over the top. Messi took it in stride, let it settle, and finished cleanly. The movement was so neat that it almost seemed to arrive from a different tournament altogether, one without the previous chaos. Yet even that goal was not enough to carry Argentina through easily. Nigeria equalized from the penalty spot. The match narrowed again. Only in the 86th minute did Marcos Rojo force the ball in and push Argentina into the knockout stage with a 2-1 win. Their life was saved. Their shape was not.

## Kazan Turned the Collapse into Reality

On June 30, in Kazan, France were waiting.

Argentina did not fail to hit back. That was part of what made the night hurt. Antoine Griezmann put France ahead from the penalty spot, but Angel Di Maria replied with a strike from distance. Early in the second half, Messi turned and shot, and the ball deflected off Gabriel Mercado into the net. For a few minutes, Argentina were ahead 2-1, and the match looked as if it had swung back under their hands.

Then the collapse came in a rush. Benjamin Pavard's volley dragged France level. Kylian Mbappe scored once, then again. 4-2. Argentina did not lose the game slowly. They lost it in the very moment when it had briefly seemed possible to grab it back. Messi kept dropping deep for the ball and pushing it forward again, but France's speed stayed at Argentina's back all the way through the closing minutes. Sergio Aguero's late header made it 4-3, but that goal only stretched the last breath of the match a little longer. It did not bring the night back.

What made Russia especially painful was how neatly it joined the defeats that had come before. The corridor at the Maracana in 2014. Santiago in 2015. MetLife in 2016. Now Kazan in 2018. When Messi had returned to the national team after saying he wanted to leave, he had done it because Argentine football already had enough problems, and he did not want to become another one. After France, the price of that return looked clearer than ever. You came back not to erase the old debt. You came back to keep wearing it.

## What Dawn Left Behind Was the Pair of Boots

The next morning, the World Cup was over, but the question it left behind was not. Russia grew light early. The corridors outside the dressing room emptied. Suitcases were dragged away one by one. By the door, a pair of boots still carried bits of grass from the night before. There was no next match, but training had not truly ended. The old group would keep breaking apart. Rebuilding would come sooner or later. The same road would gather again.

After the World Cup, what stayed first was not an answer.

It was that pair of boots by the door, and a captain who now looked older than before.

## Important Characters

### Lionel Messi
- Summary: The older captain who drags Argentina into Russia and then has to wear another failure on top of the old ones.
- Current Status: He has saved qualification with a hat-trick, only to carry a new World Cup defeat against France.

### Jorge Sampaoli
- Summary: The coach who takes over during the most unstable stretch of the cycle.
- Current Status: He has made it to Russia with the team, but he cannot make it coherent under maximum pressure.

### Argentina
- Summary: A team that still needs Messi to kick the door open first.
- Current Status: It has stumbled into the World Cup and then fallen back out without finding a durable shape.
