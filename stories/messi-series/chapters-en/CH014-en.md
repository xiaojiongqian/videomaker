# CH014 The Old Captain's Boots

## The Rebuild Began with Subtraction

In September 2018, in Los Angeles, the first Argentina training session after the World Cup began with a row of kit bags and boots set along the edge of the grass. Studs pointed outward. The names and numbers did not look like the old ones anymore. Jorge Sampaoli was gone. Lionel Scaloni and Pablo Aimar had been placed in temporary charge. The most striking change was not at the bench, though. It was by the door. The most familiar pair of boots was not there.

Russia had pushed Argentina all the way back to the training ground. There was no next World Cup match, no immediate answer, only a team whose old core was beginning to scatter and one question nobody could avoid: if Messi was not there for a while, could the blue and white shirt still stand like a team at all?

The first thing that happened in the restart was subtraction. The squad grew lighter and younger. Because Scaloni and Aimar were only meant to carry it to the end of the year, they could not pretend to unveil some grand philosophy for the future. Their work was smaller and harder than that. They had to tighten the training, tighten the order of appearances, and let new names begin to stand upright inside the shirt. The matches in September, then the two against Mexico in November, mattered for the scoreline, of course. But something else mattered more. Argentina were trying to learn how to arrange the daily work of a team without depending on one rescue at the end.

Absence did not mean forgetting. Through the rest of 2018, Messi stayed away from the national team. The grass from Russia still seemed to be stuck to the boots that had not returned. By the time Argentina beat Mexico twice in November, people were beginning to say that Scaloni had at least built something that looked like a team. But even that feeling was fragile. It looked more like an outline on a training pitch than a real answer under pressure. Everyone knew that once competitive matches came back, the old questions would narrow toward the same player again.

## Even the Return Began with Wobble

On March 7, 2019, Messi was named in the Argentina squad again for friendlies against Venezuela and Morocco. More than eight months had passed since the 4-3 defeat to France in Kazan. The empty place by the door no longer looked empty on the team sheet. But once the match against Venezuela arrived in Madrid on March 22, the return refused to become a miracle. Argentina lost 3-1. The most familiar pair of boots was back, but the team did not suddenly become whole just because he had stepped into it again. If the next Argentina side wanted to go anywhere, it could not keep asking Messi to drag matches back from broken places by himself. It had to begin by looking like a team first.

In June, the Copa America started in Brazil. Argentina lost 2-0 to Colombia, then went behind against Paraguay before Messi equalized from the penalty spot for 1-1. It was another familiar narrow gate. In earlier years, this was the kind of point when every voice began pressing in the same direction. Wait for him. Let him fix it. Yet the real step forward in that tournament was not that Messi found one more decisive kick. It was that Argentina did not fall apart there. They beat Qatar 2-0 to get through the group. They beat Venezuela 2-0 in the quarter-final. Then they lost 2-0 to Brazil in the semi-final and dropped into another small final. Against Chile in the third-place match, they won 2-1.

That was not the trophy people truly wanted. It was not a final answer either. But it felt like a plank being nailed firmly onto a road that had been shaking for years.

## The Old Captain's Weight Moved into Training

The more important change, though, happened between matches. After Russia, what the national team needed most was not the next heroic individual performance. It was a routine that could repeat every day. Who stayed. Who kept showing up. Who knew where to stand even when there was no final whistle and no penalty spot to pull the whole night tight. On July 30, the federation confirmed that Scaloni would remain in charge for the World Cup cycle ahead. The announcement was not glamorous. It felt more like a late admission. The first thing Argentina had managed to save was not glory.

It was order.

That is what changed the meaning of the boots by the door. After France, they had looked like the remains of another failure. By 2019, they were becoming something harder and slower than that. The old captain was still there, and the old question was still there with him, but the national team was no longer only waiting for him to kick the door open during matches. It was beginning to rebuild the training, the lineups, and the daily rhythm around him.

The old captain's boots did not first learn how to celebrate.

They learned how to come back the next day.

## Important Characters

### Lionel Messi
- Summary: A captain who returns, while also learning that returning no longer means repairing everything at once.
- Age & Appearance: 31; bearded, harder in the facial lines, returning as a quieter but heavier old captain.
- Current Status: He is back in the squad, but what he needs now is a team that grows around him rather than collapses toward him.

### Lionel Scaloni
- Summary: A coach who begins as a temporary figure and ends up rescuing the order of the rebuild first.
- Age & Appearance: 40; lean, short-haired, stubbled, wearing a training kit with some of his old player sharpness still visible.
- Current Status: He has earned the right to continue by stabilizing training and daily routine before anything glamorous.

### Pablo Aimar
- Summary: A quiet but concrete training-ground presence during the reset.
- Age & Appearance: 39; slim, soft-faced, short-haired, with the calm of someone who demonstrates rhythm rather than talks it.
- Current Status: He is helping Scaloni turn the post-World Cup vacuum into repeatable daily work.
