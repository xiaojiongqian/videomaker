# CH006 People Started Comparing Him to the Ones Before Him

On October 16, 2004, before the derby against Espanyol, the seventeen-year-old Messi sat on Barcelona's bench for an official match for the first time. He wore the number 30 shirt, his training top draped over his legs, but his eyes barely left the pitch. Deco scored early and Barcelona led 1-0. Yet for the young player sitting on the bench, the score was secondary. What he was really waiting for was whether that substitution board would light up for him.

Before this, he had already played in a first-team friendly, and he had already appeared in adult football with Barca C and Barca B. But none of that had yet placed him inside Barcelona's first-team league record. The new threshold in front of him was that substitution board.

Minute by minute, time kept moving. One player stood up to warm up, then another sat back down again. Messi did not fidget. He only kept watching the coaching area. Then, in the 82nd minute, the fourth official finally raised the board. Deco off. Messi on. Someone beside him tapped him once, but he was already up and running toward the line. Eight minutes is not long, but it was long enough to write his name into Barcelona's first-team league record for the first time. When he actually crossed the line, that evening finally became real.

But that appearance only pushed the door open a crack. For nearly seven months afterward, Messi still had to wait for the next chance that could truly put his name into the first team's results book. That moment finally arrived on May 1, 2005, against Albacete. Barcelona led 1-0 late in the game, and in the 88th minute Rijkaard sent Messi on for Eto'o.

In the 89th minute, Ronaldinho chipped the ball into the box. Messi ran onto it and lifted his finish past the goalkeeper. The ball went in, and the assistant's flag went up too. Offside. Goal disallowed. The celebration rose and collapsed at once. The record brushed against him, then was snatched away on the spot. Messi turned and ran back without even lifting his head much, leaving himself only two words: "Again."

In stoppage time, in the 91st minute, the ball flew from Ronaldinho's foot one more time. This time Messi timed the run again, arrived again, and lifted it over the goalkeeper again. Now it counted. The whole pitch seemed to flare up at once. Ronaldinho reached him first, laughing as he wrapped the teenager in an embrace, and the others came rushing in after him. That goal made Messi the youngest scorer in La Liga history at the time, and for the first time truly locked his name to a first-team official result for Barcelona.

He now had an official debut and an official goal, and the outside noise followed quickly. In the summer of 2005, Argentina won the World Youth Championship, and Messi's performances there carried attention out beyond Barcelona. When he came back to the club, more newspapers with his name on them began appearing outside the training ground and beside the dressing room. Someone handed one to him. The headline had put him next to Maradona. Messi looked down once, then folded the paper back up again, not letting those lines stay in his hands for long.

By the end of September 2005, even UEFA had begun publicly describing him as "the new Maradona." From the friendly debut, to the official debut, to the official first goal, those three thresholds had finally been crossed.

But having a bigger name pasted onto him first did not mean he had already secured a steadier place.

The next thing that lit up over his head would no longer be only a substitution board.

## Important Characters

### Lionel Messi
- Summary: The boy who finally reaches his official first-team debut and first official goal.
- Current Status: His name is now inside Barcelona's formal records and on the scoreboard, but his place is not fully secure yet.

### Ronaldinho
- Summary: The older star whose passing finally turns the wait into a goal that counts.
- Current Status: He has used his own gravity and generosity to push Messi into the visible center of the first team.

### Frank Rijkaard
- Summary: The coach willing to give Messi his official league entrance.
- Current Status: He has opened the senior door for real, even though Messi is still moving from prodigy to regular member.
