# CH005 Climbing One Level at a Time

At the start of the 2003/04 season, Messi was first placed in the U19B squad. In Barcelona's academy ladder at the time, the levels above that were U19A, Barca C, Barca B, and only then the first team. By age, he should first have secured his place in youth football. But almost as soon as the season began, the club kept calling him upward.

First came U19A. Against Hercules, Barcelona won 3-0, and Messi made his debut at that level. Then against Gimnastic de Tarragona, he scored. Later, against Granollers, Barcelona won 8-1 and he hit a hat-trick. The dressing rooms kept changing, the teammates kept changing, and even the shirt numbers changed with them. But one thing did not change: wherever he was placed in a match, he always found a way to leave his name on the scoreboard.

He himself seemed to be chasing those team sheets too. He might play for one side in the morning and still want to go watch another side in the afternoon. Training would be over, everyone else heading toward the showers, and Messi would still be at the side of the pitch taking a few extra touches. Someone asked him once, "Still not enough?" Messi trapped the ball against his foot, looked up, and said, "A little more." It was not a polite answer. He really did not want to be too far from the pitch.

The first time he went with the first team to Porto came down just as suddenly. Youth training had just ended that day, and Messi was getting ready to head back to the residence when the coaching staff stopped him. "Don't go back. Get your things. You're travelling with the first team." Messi froze for a second. "Now?" They nodded. He stood there, sweat still drying on him, while the road in front of him changed direction. The evening that should have taken him back to the youth team suddenly became the evening he reported to the first team instead.

On November 16, 2003, the friendly against Porto had reached its later stages when Frank Rijkaard finally gestured toward the bench. The message was obvious: warm up. Messi was on his feet almost at once, tugging his training top off as he ran toward the touchline. In the 71st minute, Fernando Navarro came off, and Messi, aged 16 years and 145 days, stepped on for his first appearance with Barcelona's first team. When he actually crossed the line, he did not stop, and he did not look back. The ground under his feet was still grass, and there was still a ball ahead of him, but this was now the touchline of the highest level inside Barca's whole system.

That night, though, only meant he had touched the first team. It did not mean he had stayed there. What the club really needed to see next was whether he could stand up in official adult football. Beneath the first team there were still two more levels: Barca C and Barca B. Thirteen days later, against Europa, Messi made his first official squad for Barca C, played all 90 minutes, and the team won 2-1.

Then came the match against Gramenet B. Barca C were still trailing 2-1 in the 88th minute. The game looked gone. In the 89th, Messi scored to make it 2-2. The ball had barely hit the net before he turned, snatched it up, and ran back, without celebrating at all. In the 90th minute, he scored again to make it 3-2. Two touches at the end of the match had turned a defeat into a win.

On March 6, 2004, against Mataro, Messi made his debut for Barca B as well and played all 90 minutes in a 1-0 win. He was moving upward so fast that it became hard for him to stay long in the same dressing room. One day it was this room, the next another. One morning he was playing with this group of teammates, and two days later he was lining up beside a different one. Someone slapped him on the shoulder and asked, "How are you everywhere?" Messi heard it, smiled once, swung his bag onto his shoulder, and headed on to the next match.

At the end of the season, he went back down to U19B for the final three league rounds and helped the team win the title. It was not a return to the beginning. It was simply because, by age, he still belonged to the youth team. The club was testing him upward while still having him finish the season he had started.

By the end of that year, the route had become very clear. He had started in U19B, surfaced with U19A, touched the touchline of a first-team friendly, then gone on to Barca C and Barca B for official adult matches, and finally returned to U19B to close out the youth season. Across the whole 2003/04 campaign, Messi played 37 official matches for four different teams and scored 35 goals, plus one first-team friendly. By then the club already knew one thing: he could not stay at his original level for long. What still had not been fully decided was where exactly they would fix him in the season to come.
